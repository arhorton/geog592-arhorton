The data that I chose to include in this project is from the New Orleans open data portal. I wanted to better understand the relationship between the wards, the Mardi Gras routes that the festival uses every year, and the presence of greenspace in the city. I have a hypothesis that the Mardi Gras routes tend to wind through the wards that posess more greenspace so that the city is able to show off their most aesthetic assets to the tourists that come to the city for the purpose of the parade. 
The details on each of the layers are below. 
1) The first layer that I chose from the New Orleans data portal is the Greenspace layer. For the purposes of my hypothesis, wards with more greenspace I believe will be more likely to be utilized by the Mardi Gras routes as the city can show off their healthy city while also giving floats, people, and performers a place to spread out along the parade route and avoid crowd crush. This data is updated irregularly, but it was last updated in April of 2026. This layer includes information on the name of the greenspace, who has jurisdiction over it, and who owns the space. I think that the owner of the area is likely to cause a shift in its preferred and usual usage, especially for the parade routes. 
2) The second layer that I chose from the New Orleans data portal is the Mardi Gras routes. According to the website, this data was last updated in February 2026, so I can presume that these routes are reasonably up-to-date. This layer includes attributes such as the location of the parade, the parade name, the number of floats, and so on. I might use the number of floats to approximate the size of the parade for my analysis. 
3) The third and final layer that I chose from the New Orlands data portal is the Ward boundaries. As I mentioned above, I am doing a ward-based analysis, as this is the manner in which New Orleans is famous for dividing up their neighborhoods. I can hypothesize that more affluent neighborhoods, which due to racism, are predominantly occupied by white individuals and families, posess more green space and thus are more likely to contain Mardi Gras routes. 
All three of these layers were sourced from the New Orleans Open Data Portal.
